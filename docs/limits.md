# Limits and details

This page holds the detail behind the short "What is not claimed" list in the README. Skip this page unless you will read the code: the internal names in it (settings, functions, test names) are there so a reader can find them.

**Known gaps** are in the last bullet of "Load and limits" below.

**The short version, in plain words:**

1. The desk is for one computer and one process. It has no login and listens on this computer only unless you change that.
2. Every call to the AI step, the CRM and the mail system is cut off by the desk after a short time (9 seconds at most). A call that is cut off is not stopped: it may still finish, and its late answer is thrown away.
3. At most a few calls of each kind run at once. If they all hang, no follow-up gets past that step until one ends; nothing is lost and nothing is sent.
4. On a blind mail system a follow-up that may or may not have been sent is marked UNKNOWN and waits for a person; a wrong answer by that person can cause a second email.
5. A computer clock that jumps by 30 seconds or more can make the desk ask again for a follow-up that was still being sent; on a keyed mail system that is harmless, on a blind one the follow-up becomes UNKNOWN.
6. A follow-up that is put back five times in a row ends `failed` even though no email was sent.
7. Real mail systems, real CRMs, real AI models, 10,000-request loads, power loss and a penetration test were not tested. Only `scripts/hubspot_probe.py` touched a real service (one free HubSpot account, logs not shipped); everything else, including the desk's HubSpot adapter, ran against stand-ins.
8. Beyond about 150 simultaneous connections on the Windows computer used, a sender that cannot retry may see refused connections; the desk never sees those.
9. Everything below was measured once, on one Windows computer, with made-up data (one kill test below was tried in a Linux container, and says so). Measured figures that no shipped command reproduces are tagged "tried once by the author, logs not shipped"; their raw logs are not part of this repository.
10. None of this is legal advice or a guarantee.

## What is not claimed (in full)

* "Never silently lost" and "never sent twice" in general. Exactly-once delivery, production fitness, a legal opinion.
  The licence text has not been reviewed by a lawyer.
* The same person with two email addresses is two leads: `ada+sales@example.com` and `a.da@example.com` are different leads.
  Two people sharing one address are one lead.
* A real mail system or CRM was not tested (except by the one probe in `docs/hubspot-probe.md`). The keyed mail system is a simulation; a real provider may remember a send key for a
  shorter or longer time. The AI step is a set of pre-written replies; nothing is said about real AI behaviour.
* **Live model step.** The desk can ask a real AI model for a label, but only when you set `DESK_QUALIFIER=live_claude`, name a key file
  and set a price, a per-run cap and a total cap (see `docs/settings.md`). Out of the box (the default `recorded` setting, which means pre-written replies) no model is called and nothing leaves
  the computer. The live step was run only against a local stand-in server; no real provider was called while this was written.
* **Every call to the AI step, the CRM and the mail system is bounded by the desk itself** (`Desk._bounded`): it runs in a helper thread
  and the desk waits for it at most `MODEL_CALL_BUDGET_SECONDS` (9 s) for the AI step and at most `min(send_timeout_seconds, 9 s)` for a
  CRM put, a send or a capability read. A call that is late is abandoned, not stopped: it keeps running until it ends on its own, its late
  answer is thrown away and never reaches the desk's records, and the reaper carries on with other rows. What a timeout means for a row:
  an AI timeout uses up one of the 5 automatic tries (`internal:ModelTimeout`; a person's Retry starts the count again at try 1, so one lead can be tried more than 5 times in all); a keyed send or CRM put is asked again later with the same
  key; a blind send or CRM put ends the row `unknown` for a person to look at, and a blind call is not started again for the same lead
  while its old call is still running. Tests: `test_reaper_survives_hung_model`, `test_hung_model_spends_the_try`,
  `test_late_answer_writes_nothing`, `test_blind_send_timeout_is_unknown`, `test_keyed_send_timeout_same_key`,
  `test_blind_crm_timeout_is_unknown`, `test_late_send_writes_nothing`, `test_late_crm_put_writes_nothing`.
* **At most 4 AI calls, 8 CRM puts, 8 sends and 4 capability reads run at once** (`ADAPTER_WORKER_CAPS`; abandoned calls count until they
  end, the place is reserved before the helper thread starts). When a kind is full, or a blind call for the same lead is still running, the
  row is not claimed: it keeps its age and shows up as `stalled` in the review list after `stall_seconds` (`test_busy_give_back_keeps_the_age`,
  `test_full_model_cap_shows_the_row`, `test_blind_call_for_lead_blocks_claim`). `/counts` and the portal summary show
  `hung_workers`. If four AI calls hang for ever, no follow-up gets past the AI step until one ends or the desk restarts; rows stay visible
  and nothing is sent or lost. An abandoned call keeps its socket and, with a paid model, may still be charged.
* **A person cannot answer `not_sent` while a call for that lead is still running** (409 `call_still_running`): the old call may still
  land, so "nothing there yet" proves nothing. Wait until `hung_workers` is back to 0, look again, then answer (`test_blind_retry_waits_for_old_send`).
* **Time budget.** A `sending` row is put back by the reaper only when it is strictly older than the stuck grace: the send timeout plus 5
  database waits of 5 s, 30 s with the defaults. One stage must fit inside it: the call budget (9 s) plus the longest write of the stamp that
  follows (15 s: the 10 s write deadline plus one more database wait) is 24 s, strictly below the smallest grace of 25 s. A constant that does not
  hold this ends with a Python error (exit code 1) when the program loads. An AI adapter that declares a `timeout_seconds` that is not
  a number above 0, or above 9 s, is refused when the desk is built (exit code 4).
* **A lock after the model answered.** If the database stays locked when the answer is stamped or its label written, the answer is thrown away
  and the try stays used up (`internal:ModelAnswerLost`), because the model was really asked; the 5-try limit then bounds how often it is
  asked again (`test_locked_stamp_cannot_ask_for_ever`, `test_answer_lost_real_lock_keeps_try`). A lock before the model is asked gives the try back.
* **A late answer from an abandoned mode read** (the capability of the CRM or the mail system) cannot overwrite a newer one: each read carries
  its start time and an older one is ignored (`test_late_refresh_keeps_newer_mode`).
* A stopped desk does not wait for its handler threads or for a follow-up that is still running: that is the crash case (the stuck row is
  put back at the next start; the ten crash points cover it). A client that sends one byte every 1.9 seconds can hold a connection
  place for the whole header and body deadlines; this costs availability only (nothing is stored) and is deliberate.
* The desk assumes the follow-up is the only thing that sends mail for a lead's send key.
* A crash is simulated inside the program (the test raises an exception at the point) for all ten crash points and for the eleventh, N1 (the reminder email, `tests/test_reminder_email.py`). A real process kill (the desk process ends at once, no clean-up) at a NAMED crash point was done only at D2: twice with the demo mail system over HTTP (keyed and blind), and once with a real mail catcher process that receives mail over SMTP (blind sender).
  * A second kind of real kill, at a moment nobody chose, was tried in a Linux container: the real desk process was ended once while a load ran (the script that did this and its logs are not shipped; the load driver and the after-load checks are, see `load_tools/README.md`). In every one of those runs every follow-up ended `sent`, so they back the after-load checks and the retry story, not the UNKNOWN path. Power loss was not tested.
* Loads of 10,000 requests were not run, and none of the burst figures in "Load and limits" were measured on a second Windows computer. The desk is for one computer and one process. There is no login: it listens on this computer only unless you change `DESK_BIND`, and then anyone who can reach that address can read the desk's records (see "The portal and the desk process" below).
* Run on two systems: Windows with Python 3.13 (the Windows lock file) and Linux with Python 3.12 (a Docker container, the Linux lock file). The full test suite was run on both on 2026-11-12: Windows with Python 3.13 2449 passed, 15 skipped; Linux with Python 3.12 (a container) 2462 passed, 2 skipped; no test failed on either (the logs are not shipped). The skipped tests need another operating system, a tool that machine did not have, or the load measurement. Edits made after those runs changed only documentation and one test file, which was run on its own. The checks in `.github/workflows/lint.yml` are configured, but no run of them is claimed here. The load figures were not measured on another operating system.
* n8n crashing before the request reaches the desk. Other n8n versions or set-ups: no n8n-only result is claimed here: the n8n tries were one pattern, one computer, made-up data, tried once.
* Reminders (when the lead owner feature is on): a lead is reminded once after the wait if its step is still before `reminder_until`, even if its follow-up email has not been sent yet, because the reminder asks whether the lead moved on, not whether the email was sent. A lead created while the feature was off has no owner, step or reminder, and the step route answers `no_state` for it; only leads created after the switch are covered. A reminder is never sent a second time: one that failed or could not be confirmed is listed for a person.
* A person answering "not sent" wrongly for an UNKNOWN follow-up on a mail system that cannot recognise repeats (then a second email can go).
  UNKNOWN items stay unseen unless someone opens the review list.
* A follow-up stuck in `sending` (for example because the database stayed locked through all retries) is put back by the desk's background job (the "reaper", started by `main`, one pass every second) when it has been stuck for more than 30 seconds by the computer's calendar clock (a clock step of 30 s or more forward can put back a follow-up that is still being sent: on a keyed mail system it is sent again with the same key, on a blind one it becomes `unknown`; after a step backward the reaper sets the stamp of a pending or sending row that lies in the future to the new time, so such a row ages normally and is put back 30 s later; `_restamp_future` in `src/lead_desk/desk.py`, `tests/test_clock_steps.py`): to `pending` on a keyed mail system, to `unknown` on a blind one, when the send may have started.
  * Exceptions, case by case: a follow-up that never sent anything (the CRM step was not finished, or the send mark was not written) goes to `pending` on either kind of mail system, or to `failed` on either kind when it has already used all 5 automatic tries (`SEND_MAX_ATTEMPTS`); a follow-up whose send may have started goes to `pending` (or `failed` at the cap) on a keyed system and to `unknown` on a blind one.
  * A person's Retry starts a `failed` follow-up again. A lock on a write after the claim normally gives the automatic try back (the follow-up goes to `pending` at once), except a lock that stops the write of a model answer (`ModelAnswerLost`: the try stays used up); a row stays `sending` for the reaper only when the give-back write is locked too (the database stayed locked through all retries of that write as well).
  * The put-back does not give the try back, so a follow-up that is put back five times in a row (the give-back stayed locked each time, or a drive was held up past the grace) ends `failed` although no email was sent and the CRM was not written (the AI step may have been asked). Restarting the desk does the same at once.
  * One reaper pass may start new follow-ups for at most `REAPER_PASS_SECONDS` (10 s) and then overruns by the one follow-up it is driving (at most three bounded adapter calls of at most 9 s, plus its database writes) and by its two capability reads at the start (two deadlines): 10 + 27 + 18 = 55 s at the very worst with the defaults and no lock trouble (so a pass can take longer than the 30 s grace; the grace is measured per row, not per pass). `stop_background` waits only its own timeout (10 s); a pass that is still running ends by itself at its next check and the desk keeps track of it until it has ended (`test_stop_waits_only_its_timeout`).
* No penetration test of the desk was done.

## Counters and database rules

What `/counts` adds up: `received = new + duplicate + review + open` (`open` = a receipt with no verdict yet). A request the desk refused before a receipt existed (bad or too large) is not in `received`; it is counted apart in `rejected_envelopes`, which lives in the desk's memory and starts again at 0 after a restart. There is no "failed" verdict for a request; a follow-up that failed is counted apart (`failed_followups`). Two more counters live in the desk's memory and start at 0 after a restart: `decide_transient_errors` (a database fault that is not a lock, such as a disk error, met while deciding a receipt; the receipt stays open, the request gets an error reply, the reaper tries again at every pass, and no verdict is written for it) and `decide_integrity_errors` (a broken database rule; the receipt gets the verdict `review` with the reason `internal_unstorable:integrity`, and `ok` in `/counts` is false while this counter is above 0). A disk fault was only simulated in tests, never a real full disk. Test: `test_reconcile_counts`.

Rules of the desk's records (the database refuses these, for a database created by this version): a receipt cannot be changed or deleted, a verdict is written once, and a verdict `new` must name its lead (`test_write_once_triggers`); a lead cannot be changed or deleted (same test); a follow-up line cannot be deleted or replaced by `INSERT OR REPLACE`, and a `sent` follow-up cannot be changed (same test). A desk's records that lacks one of the database rules (the "triggers") is repaired when the desk starts; this is tested in `tests/test_migration_triggers.py`, which also drops the follow-up delete rule and checks it is back. Not proven by any test: the checks that SQLite cannot add to an existing table (the CHECK on receipts that makes a verdict `new` name its lead) exist only in books created by this version.

## Load and limits

* Burst test: many connections open at the same moment, all for the same lead (the same email under different request numbers). To repeat a small one, start the demo (README, "Try it") and run `python -m demo_only.burst --url http://127.0.0.1:8765/leads --n 100 --email ada@example.com --ref-prefix run1 --source form` (with `PYTHONPATH=src:.`). Read the counts it prints: for one lead sent many times, the lead count and the follow-up count should each be 1 (`tests/test_http.py::test_ten_parallel` checks this for 10).
* Larger bursts: one measurement test is shipped, `LEAD_DESK_LOAD_N=300 python -m pytest tests/test_server_hardening.py -k measure_burst -s -p no:cacheprovider` (add `LEAD_DESK_LOAD_RETRY=1` to let the sender retry a refused connection). It prints how many connections the operating system refused and the status counts. On the Windows computer of the author, 150 simultaneous connections were all answered (tried once by the author, logs not shipped); from about 300 upward the operating system refused part of the connections before the desk saw them (a plain stand-alone web server on the same laptop did the same), and the counts varied from run to run. The waiting line is 1024 connections (`LISTEN_QUEUE` in `src/lead_desk/constants.py`) and the operating system may allow fewer. These counts depend on the computer and its operating system; they are not claimed for any other machine, and the author's raw logs are not part of this repository.
  * So beyond about 150 simultaneous connections on that computer a sender that cannot retry may see "connection refused" for part of the burst, and nothing in the desk records it, because the desk never saw it.
* **Retry rule of the senders in this repository.** The burst script, the portal demo's burst and the load driver (`load_tools/load_driver.py`) retry a refused connection or a 503 by TIME, not by count: the first wait is 0.1 s, each wait doubles up to 1 s, the desk's `Retry-After` is honoured up to that 1 s, and no new try starts more than 30 s after the first (`BURST_BACKOFF_SECONDS`, `BURST_BACKOFF_MAX_SECONDS`, `BURST_RETRY_DEADLINE_SECONDS` in `constants.py`; tests in `tests/test_burst_retry.py`). `max_retries=0` in the burst script means no retry at all.
* Of the connections the desk did receive in the larger bursts, some were answered 503 `busy` (database lock wait over). A 503 from a lock wait may have left an open receipt; a 503 from the connection limit never does. In both cases send the same request number again. The desk answered every received connection with JSON, and the burst command and `tests/test_http.py::test_ten_parallel` check that only one email is sent for the lead.
* A request body shorter than the Content-Length it announced: the desk answers 408 `request_timeout` when the body has not fully arrived 10 seconds after the headers ended (`BODY_READ_DEADLINE_SECONDS`) if the sender keeps the connection open, or 400 if the sender closes it; the 408 and the 400 are both counted in `rejected_envelopes` when the stall or close happens in the body of `POST /leads`; a stall while the headers are still arriving gets 408 but is not counted. A Content-Length over the size cap, however long the number, is answered 413 straight away; this holds for the decision body of `POST /review/<id>/resolve` too (after the Host and Origin check). Two different Content-Length values in one request are answered 400. A request target that cannot be parsed is answered 400.
* **Known gaps in these figures.** The larger bursts depend on the machine and were not repeated for every version of the desk or with every sender retry rule; treat them as indications for the machine they ran on.

## The portal and the desk process

**Limits and shutdown of the desk process.** The idle-connection behaviour below is covered by the named test.

* Portal: `/api/ui` answers at most 8 requests at once; more get 503 `busy`. The count "Requests turned away before they were stored" on the Overview page (`refused_at_door`: requests refused as a bad envelope, too large, a body that timed out or a bad request reference, counted as `rejected_envelopes`; a 403 from the Host and Origin check is not counted; not the same as the source of a lead, `form` or `webhook`, called `door` in the data) is kept in memory and starts again at 0 when the desk restarts.
* The portal cannot tell a stopped desk from a lost network connection between the browser and the desk: both show the notice "The desk is offline" with the last values and paused buttons. Check that the desk process runs before you look at the network.
* **Later messages.** The lead panel lists the later messages of a lead: requests with a new request number and an email address the desk already has. It compares each one with the first record only, field by field (name, phone, company, message), and lists a field when the new value is not empty and differs. Not claimed:
  * A later message that went to "Request to check" (for example an invalid email address or a field that is not text) has no lead and never appears under "Later messages"; it is on Needs a person.
  * An empty field cannot clear a value. A change made only of extra space characters between words is not listed (runs of the space character count as one); a change of upper or lower case is listed. Other fields a sender adds are stored with the request but not listed.
  * A later message that repeats a value an earlier later message already sent is listed again.
  * The same request number sent again with different data is not a later message: the first answer stands, the new data is not stored, and it stays on Needs a person as "Same request number, different data".
  * Only the newest 50 later messages of a lead are shown, with the total; older ones stay stored but the panel does not show them. The list part of the panel's answer is at most 3,132,800 bytes (50 later messages, every field at its longest in the characters that take the most bytes); the whole answer also carries the lead and its open items.
  * Times are the desk's clock at arrival in whole seconds, not the sender's time. A later message whose request reached the desk before the first record was written (two requests at almost the same moment) is shown as "Arrived before the first record was written". When the desk clock gives a later message an earlier time than the first message, no gap is shown. Durations are rounded down to whole seconds, minutes, hours or days; the exact seconds are in the tooltip.
  * Approval mode (`DESK_LATER_MODE=approval`, off by default) records one person's choice about a later message and nothing else: the approver is a name picked from the list and is not verified, and nothing is executed by the choice. The desk does not accept, dismiss or answer a later message on its own, sends no notification and no second reply, and handles no later message automatically or with an AI step; suggested replies are not built. Nothing from a later message reaches the CRM or the follow-up email.
  * Email addresses, phone numbers, messages, lead names and request numbers are masked on screen by default (each with its own eye button; a request number is data the sender chose, so it is treated as personal until shown on purpose; companies, lead owners and approver names are shown as they are). A company name can itself be a person's name (a sole trader), and it is shown and searched while names are hidden: that is a written rule with a known residual risk, and a sender who must not be recognisable should not put a name in the company field; the "Hide names and email addresses" switch, when off, shows them and allows searching by name and by email address (while they are masked the search matches only the company and the lead number, so nobody can probe whether a name exists, not even through a request number). A small eye button beside a masked value shows that one value only: it is kept in memory (never stored), hides again after 30 seconds, on Escape, when the panel closes or the page changes, and when the switch is turned on, and it does not turn on searching by name or address. On the demo controls page the lead pick list shows "Lead 1", "Lead 2" and so on while masking is on. The switch choice is kept for the browser tab's session only (session storage, not local storage), so a new session starts masked and a shown state cannot be left behind; a browser that blocks site storage starts masked and keeps a choice only for that visit. An empty phone or message shows as "empty" also when masked, so a viewer can see whether one was given. A masked email address shows as "•••@•••••" (no character of it is kept).
  * Masking is on screen only. The answer the page reads still carries every value, lead names included (the browser's developer tools show it; checked in tests/test_portal_lead_pages.py), and anyone who can reach the desk can call `/api/ui/leads?by_email=1` and search by name or email address: the switch decides only what the pages ask for and show. The desk's own log lines print no email address, phone number or message (every log call is checked by a test); text that the Python runtime itself writes to the error output when a thread fails unexpectedly is covered only on the paths the tests run. Tests: `test_later_values_word_for_word`, `test_later_lower_receipt_number`, `test_later_review_verdict_not_listed`, `test_later_repeat_still_listed`, `test_later_reply_size_is_bounded`, `test_leads_email_search_needs_reveal`, `test_personal_hidden_rule`, `test_reveal_one_value_rules`, `test_log_calls_carry_no_values`, `test_later_messages_log_no_values`.
* Names, email addresses, phone numbers and messages are masked on screen by default; switch the masking off in the Leads page ("Hide names and email addresses") to see them and to search by name or address. The masking is a display choice of that browser: the JSON replies of the desk still carry the values (browser developer tools show them), and the desk's logs never hold them (one test sends canary values and searches the log output).
* Threads: the desk serves each connection on its own thread. Writing a reply has a total limit of `REPLY_WRITE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` (5 s): a client that does not read its reply loses the thread after that.
* Open files: at start-up the desk checks that the open-file limit of this computer covers `MAX_HANDLER_THREADS` (4 files per thread plus 96 for everything else; contract.md lists what the 4 and the 96 are made of). With the default 1024 threads that is 4192 open files. A computer whose hard limit is lower than that makes the desk exit with code 3; 4096 is a common default inside containers, so there raise the open-file limit of the operating system (for example `ulimit -n 8192`, or the container's own open-file setting).
  * The count is arithmetic, checked by measuring a database connection and the portal's connections; DNS lookups and the temporary files SQLite makes are covered by the 96 only roughly (not measured, not itemised). It raises the soft limit to exactly what is needed when the hard limit is unlimited or at least that, and otherwise refuses to start (exit code 3). The message names the open-file limit in force and what the desk needs.
* Exit codes: 1 the sender cannot be reached, or a Python traceback (a constant that is out of range); 2 another desk holds the lock file; 3 the open-file limit is too low; 4 the desk refuses a setting (a number that is not plain digits or is outside its range, any other bad `DESK_*` value, or an AI adapter whose `timeout_seconds` is not a number above 0 or is above the AI call budget of 9 s; the message names the setting; an error that is not a refused setting never gets code 4); 5 the port could not be bound within `BIND_RETRY_SECONDS` (see above).
* Stopping: Ctrl+C or SIGTERM stop the background job first and then give the lock file back. A second signal while that runs is ignored on purpose, so it cannot cut a step short. If the desk is still there after about 10 seconds, end it with SIGKILL (on Windows, end the process); the lock is dropped and the next start puts every unfinished follow-up back.
* Two limits apply. (1) At most `MAX_CONNECTIONS` (256) connections can be in their reading phase (request line, headers, body) at one time; a connection stops counting when the desk starts writing its reply, so a thread that is still finishing a follow-up does not count here. (2) At most `MAX_HANDLER_THREADS` (1024) handler threads exist in total: a thread counts from the moment it gets a connection until it ends, so the reply write, the draining and the follow-up count too. When either limit is reached and nothing can be closed to make room, the new connection gets 503 `busy` and nothing is stored.
* Idle connections: a client that opens connections and sends nothing holds a place until the header deadline (10 s), then gets 408 and is closed (`test_idle_sockets_closed_at_deadline`). While they hold the 256 places, a valid request gets 503 `busy` (that is the backpressure; send it again).
* Deadlines: request line plus headers must arrive within `HEADER_READ_DEADLINE_SECONDS` and the body within `BODY_READ_DEADLINE_SECONDS` (10 s each, measured in real time from the start of that phase, however slowly the bytes trickle in); a stalled sender gets 408 `{"error":"request_timeout","receipt_id":null}`, and nothing was stored, so the same request number can be sent again.
* Making room: when 256 connections are being read, the oldest one that is still being read (headers or body) and has sent no byte for `EVICT_MIN_IDLE_SECONDS` (2 s) is closed with no reply (its sender sees a dropped connection, nothing was stored, and it must send the request again; the same request number is safe). A connection that keeps sending is never closed this way.
  * A connection is also safe from the moment its whole request has been read, parsed and checked (JSON and envelope) and the desk starts acting on it. When none can be closed, the new connection gets 503 `{"error":"busy","reason":"busy"}` with `Retry-After: 1`. That 503 is written before any receipt exists, and it is a different 503 from the ones for a database lock (`/leads`) and for more than 8 portal requests at once (`/api/ui`, body `{"error":"busy"}`).
* Keep the desk off networks you do not trust. A bind written as a short all-zeros form (for example the single digit 0) is treated as a wildcard. Close the portal while you run a load test: every open page asks the desk for its numbers every few seconds.

## What the checks do not cover

The scan for hardcoded literals is a safety net, not a guarantee. Look by eye for: compressed IPv6 addresses (for example `fe80::1`),
host names that end in a word not in the scan's list, UNC paths (`\\server\share`), and bare port numbers. Numbers and ports are NOT checked by that
scan; they live in `src/lead_desk/config.py` and `src/lead_desk/constants.py`. Base64-encoded words are not covered by the optional scan for caller-supplied terms. The list of allowed matches (`exceptions.txt`) allows test function names of 40 or more characters anywhere under `tests/`, because the scan reports only the matched text and cannot tell a `def test_...` line from other text. Whole-file allowances (the web addresses in `constants.py`, the 40- and 64-digit hashes in the workflow and lock files) are not checked line by line by the scan.

## When the copy of a lead stage to the CRM fails

The copy of a lead's stage (and owner) to the CRM is tried again, not given up at once: after a failed try the desk waits 10, 60, 180 and then 420 seconds before the next one (five tries in all, about 11 minutes, counted on the desk's own clock, so after a restart the next try happens immediately; the number of tries already made is kept, so the waits continue from that point and the five tries in all still apply). After the fifth failure the desk gives up on that stage: the lead's side panel shows the chip "Not copied" with a note, and the desk does not try again by itself. Only a later stage restarts the copy: moving the lead to a later step (by hand, or the automatic step after the follow-up) resets the count. A lead that already is at the last stage has no later step, so it is not copied again; the note says so. The tests are in `tests/test_mirror_gate.py`.

## Which CRM stage things the desk touches

> **WARNING: this write can start automations in the CRM you connect.** When lead stages are on, the desk writes the contact's lead status field (in HubSpot `hs_lead_status`). Any automation that watches that field (a workflow, sequence, lead rotation, scoring rule, notification, integration or report) may react to our write, for example by emailing the contact, creating a task, changing the owner or sending a chat alert. The desk neither sees nor controls those automations, and it does not know what the automations of the CRM's owner do. The feature is OFF unless `DESK_LEADFLOW_FILE` is set to a file; the only values the desk writes are the lead status values named in that file's stage list. **Required before you point this at a real CRM:** the CRM admin lists every automation that reacts to lead status changes and confirms each one is safe or switched off, and the first test uses one made-up contact. The desk has no sign-in, so any program that can reach its port could cause this write (the step route) or create contacts and send mail (`POST /leads`, the review routes). With any real service set (a real CRM, the real mail sender or the live AI step) the desk therefore starts only on a loopback address and refuses any other `DESK_BIND`.

A CRM keeps several different "stage" fields. The desk writes only one of them, and only when a lead stage file (`DESK_LEADFLOW_FILE`) is switched on.

| CRM field | What the desk does | Side effects in your HubSpot |
|---|---|---|
| **Lead stage** (the desk's own wording) = in HubSpot the contact property "lead status" (`hs_lead_status`) | **Written.** Each lead stage in the file names the lead status value to copy (the demo file uses `NEW`, `ATTEMPTED_TO_CONTACT`, `CONNECTED`, `OPEN_DEAL`). | **Yes, possibly.** Any automation that watches this field reacts to our write. The desk cannot see or control that. |
| Lifecycle stage (`lifecyclestage`: subscriber, lead, customer and so on) | Not touched. | None from the desk. |
| Lead pipeline stages (HubSpot's separate Leads object) | Not touched. | None from the desk. |
| Deal stages (the Deals object) | Not touched. | None from the desk. |
| Other CRMs | Only that CRM's own status field, and only if its adapter maps the lead stage to it. | Same warning: automations on that field react. |

HubSpot's default lead status options are New, Open, In Progress, Open Deal, Unqualified, Attempted to Contact, Connected and Bad Timing. An account can rename or add options, so the file must use values that exist on the account. The `docs/hubspot-probe.md` runs (the only real-service try; one free account, logs not shipped) showed one write (`OPEN`) accepted; the full list of default options and the write against our own portal are not verified.
